## Glossary {#glossary}

- **10-Q**: The quarterly report a US public company files with the SEC, the market regulator. Its figures are unaudited; the annual version is the 10-K.
- **Backlog (remaining performance obligations)**: Revenue a company has signed contracts for but not yet delivered. Accountants call it remaining performance obligations; Microsoft reported $683B at 30 September 2026 ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000119312526436133/msft-20260930.htm), unaudited).
- **Capex**: Capital expenditure, money spent on things that last for years, such as buildings, power equipment and chips, rather than on day-to-day running costs. Amazon's purchases of property and equipment were $77.3B in Q3 2026 ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000040/amzn-20260930.htm), unaudited).
- **Credit default swap**: A contract in which one side pays a fee and is paid if a borrower fails to repay its debt. A higher price means investors see more risk; the Financial Times reported on 3 November 2026 that these prices for AI borrowers had risen ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/5f08f1fb-4948-4420-8fe4-3c8c8cc492ef)).
- **Depreciation**: Spreading the cost of an asset over the years it is expected to last, instead of counting it all at once. It is a yearly expense that involves no cash leaving that year; Microsoft recorded $11.1B of property and equipment depreciation in its fiscal Q1 2027 (unaudited).
- **EBITDA**: Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, a rough measure of operating profit before the cost of borrowing and of aging assets. Many companies publish adjusted versions that they define themselves, so compare with care.
- **Finance lease**: A lease that works like buying with installments. The user records the asset and a matching debt on its books; some companies count the payments in their capex.
- **Free cash flow**: Cash from operations minus spending on capex, or what is left for debt and owners. Amazon's was negative $42.4B over the 12 months to 30 September 2026 on its own definition (unaudited).
- **GPU**: Graphics processing unit, a chip that does huge numbers of sums at the same time. It began in video graphics and now does most of the heavy work in AI.
- **GPU-hour**: One GPU rented for one hour, the usual price unit for AI computing. The median on-demand price for an NVIDIA H100 was $4.20 on 7 October 2026 ([Uvik](https://uvik.net/blog/gpu-price-index/)).
- **Hyperscaler**: A company that runs data centers at very large scale and rents out the computing inside them. The three giants, Amazon, Microsoft and Google, held 61% of cloud infrastructure in Q3 2026 ([Synergy Research Group](https://www.srgresearch.com/articles/cloud-market-growth-rate-surges-to-well-over-50-per-year-q3-2026)).
- **Inference**: Using a trained AI model to answer a question or produce a result. Each answer is small, but the total grows with every user.
- **Investment grade**: A credit rating in the upper bands, meaning the rating agency judges that default is unlikely. Borrowing is usually cheaper at these ratings.
- **Joint venture**: A shared business that two or more companies set up and fund together. Meta and Blue Owl formed one of about $27B for the Hyperion campus, with Blue Owl funding 80% ([Hubbis](https://www.hubbis.com/news/meta-and-blue-owl-capital-launch-usd27-billion-joint-venture-for-hyperion)); Meta says it does not consolidate two data center ventures in which it holds 20%.
- **MW and GW**: Units of power. A megawatt (MW) is a million watts and a gigawatt (GW) is 1,000 MW; CoreWeave reported 1.5 GW of active power at 30 June 2026 (unaudited).
- **Neocloud**: A newer company built mainly to rent out GPU computing for AI, such as CoreWeave and Nebius. Most lack the older businesses that fund the giants' building.
- **Operating margin**: Operating profit divided by revenue, or how many cents of each sales dollar is left after running costs and depreciation. AWS's was 38.7% in Q3 2026 ([Amazon release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000038/amzn-20260930xex991.htm), unaudited).
- **PUE**: Power usage effectiveness, total power drawn by a data center divided by the power reaching its computers. A perfect score is 1.0; Google reports 1.09 against an industry average of 1.54 ([Google](https://datacenters.google/efficiency/)).
- **SAM**: Serviceable addressable market, the part of the total market a business could actually serve with what it sells. This page estimates one for AI-heavy computing.
- **SOM**: Serviceable obtainable market, the slice of the SAM a business could realistically win. For a new entrant, it is a share of today's SAM over a few years; the page treats it as an estimate, not a forecast.
- **Spot capacity**: Spare cloud machines sold at a deep discount, up to 90% off on AWS, with the catch that the provider can take them back at short notice ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/ec2/spot/pricing/)).
- **TAM**: Total addressable market, the whole yearly revenue available in a market. Here it is cloud infrastructure services: $558B over the 12 months to Q3 2026 ([Synergy Research Group](https://www.srgresearch.com/articles/cloud-market-growth-rate-surges-to-well-over-50-per-year-q3-2026)).
- **Token**: A small chunk of text, roughly a word or part of one, that AI models read and write. Many AI services charge per million tokens.
- **Training**: Teaching an AI model by running it over very large amounts of data. It can take thousands of chips for weeks, then the finished model moves on to inference.
- **Useful life**: The number of years a company assumes an asset will last, which sets how fast it is depreciated. It is a judgment; Meta extended most servers to 5.5 years from 1 January 2025, which cut its 2025 depreciation by $2.92B ([10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026003942/meta-20251231.htm)).
- **Utilization**: The share of capacity actually in use. A data center full of idle chips earns little, so keeping utilization high matters.
